World War II

World War II officially started in late summer 1939 when Germany invaded and quickly took over Poland. Right after that, Central and Eastern Europe fractured, with countries either joining the Axis powers or picking a fight with Germany. Over the next few years, the conflict dragged in more than 50 nations across the globe. The massive war played out in the European and Pacific theaters, with combat on land, in the air, and across the oceans. In the years leading up to the start of the war, Germany began secretly manufacturing tanks and planes in an attempt to create an army. The war began in the summer of 1939 when Germany invaded Poland in just a few weeks. Then, a few months later, Germany invaded both Norway and Denmark. Less than a month later, Germany implemented a new strategy called “blitzkrieg” or lightning war, which they used to quickly conquer Belgium and the Netherlands. Three days later, they also began an assault on France, which lasted less than 50 days. During this battle, Italy also declared war on France and Britain. In September of 1940, Germany began sending bombing missions to Britain, and due to the Luftwaffe's air superiority, they were able to quickly and with minimal casualties execute these missions. However, in the following months Britain was able to provide more and more defence until they were able to block the attacks. Within six months Germany broke its treaty with the Soviet Union and began its attack with Hitler aiming to capture Moscow within a few months. Meanwhile, on December 7, 1941, Japan launched a surprise attack on Pearl Harbor, forcing the United States to officially enter the war. This forced the Japanese military onto the defensive as American forces began progressing towards Japan by jumping between islands. Around the same time, the Allies successfully cleared Axis forces from North Africa, setting the stage for the massive D-Day invasion of Europe. About a year after Allied troops landed in Normandy, Germany finally surrendered, leaving the defeated country completely broken and split into two separate zones, one becoming free while the other fell under the Soviet Union's control. Finally, in September of 1945, after six years, Japan surrendered, marking the end of the war.

In the book The Book Thief written by Markus Zusak, a story is portrayed about a girl who is sent to live with a family in Nazi Germany. Throughout the book the reader experiences an introduction into how the war shaped people's lives and how oppressive the Nazi regime was. With the food shortages and bombing missions this book is an extremely good example of how with enough persuasion people who may not be evil can be converted with propaganda being a leading cause of this. Ultimately, this book portrays both the cruelty and kindness expressed in people when facing danger.
