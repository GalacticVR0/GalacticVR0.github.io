Allies

The B-17 Flying Fortress was a World War Two heavy bomber designed by Boeing who produced aproximately 12500 units that were used by the US Army Air Corps. It utilizes four engines for better reliability and redundancy while also allowing it to carry a larger bomb payload of up to 8000 pounds. While on a mission it could carry a crew of up to ten people. The crew would consist of a pilot, co-pilot, navigator or radioman, bombadier, as well as several gunners. With the use of several turrets each utilizing one to two 50 caliber machine guns the B-17 had 360 degree defence from enemy fighters. The aircraft was designed to fly above the range of the German anti-aircraft weapons and if shot at would still be able to complete the mission with minimal casualties. It would typically be used for carpet bombing (dropping a large number of bombs over a wide area) but was also used for strategic bombing missions which would include bombing factories or military installations. B-17s were primarily used in the European Theater however they were for a few missions used in the Pacific Theater. It was typically escourted by P-51 Mustangs or occasionally P-47 Thunderbolts. Even though the B-17 was extremely robust and heavily protected from airial threats with both the use of turrets and escourt fighters they still suffered heavy losses during the war. On each mission during the beginning of the war about five to ten percent of the bombers were lost each mission although near the end of the war it dropped down to less than five percent.

Yamato

The Yamato was a Japanese battleship commissioned in 1941 and was the lead ship of the Yamato class. It was the largest battleship built during World War II with a length of 263 meters and a displacement of over 70000 tons. The battleship was armed with three turrets each consisting of three 18.1 inch guns which were the largest naval guns ever mounted on a boat. It first saw combat in 1942 during the Battle of Midway however it did not engage in combat. Later it participated in the Battle of Leyte Gulf in 1944 where along with its sister ship the Musashi it played a role in the counter attack against the US Navy. However during the attack the Musashi was sunk from carrier based aircraft which heavily impacted the Yamato's expected ability to fight. In 1945 the Yamato participated in its final battle known as Operation Ten-Go. During this the Yamato ocompanied by a small escourt fleet was sent to attack American forces that were landing on Okinawa. When the Yamato was soon spotted by an American Carrier Task Force it was attacked by many waves of aircraft before it was finally sunk by a torpedo. The sinking of the Yamato marked the end of an era of the dominance of battleships in naval warfare as the use of aircraft carriers became more practical and effective.
