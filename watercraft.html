AirCraft Carriers

Aircraft carriers provided a crucial advantage in naval warfare during World War II that other naval assets could not provide by acting in a support role. With one of two theatres featured in the war being fought on and around the Pacific Ocean, the use of aircraft to support ground forces or attack enemy naval assets were impractical due to the large amount of fuel needed to fly from the United States to an island closer to combat with an airfield. Due to these reasons the United States implemented carrier groups which would consist of a few aircraft carriers and many support ships. Each carrier could carry up to over 100 aircraft near the end of the war with the addition of the Midway class. Aircraft carriers were used in several famous missions during the war such as the Doolittle Raid in 1942, where 16 B-25 bombers were launched from the USS Hornet to attack Tokyo. They also played a crucial role in sinking the Japanese battleship Yamato. During the war the United States had a total of 150 active aircraft carriers at one point, resulting in the United States having the largest fleet of aircraft carriers in the world. Overall, aircraft carriers were a crucial asset to the United States as well as the rest of the Allies and was one of the reasons why the Allies won the war in the Pacific.

Yamato

The Yamato was a Japanese battleship commissioned in 1941 and was the lead ship of the Yamato class. It was the largest battleship built during World War II with a length of 263 meters and a displacement of over 70,000 tons. The battleship was armed with three turrets each consisting of three 18.1 inch guns, which were the largest naval guns ever mounted on a boat. It first saw combat in 1942 during the Battle of Midway, however it did not engage in combat. Later it participated in the Battle of Leyte Gulf in 1944 where along with its sister ship, the Musashi, it played a role in the counter attack against the US Navy. However during the attack the Musashi was sunk from carrier based aircraft, which heavily impacted the Yamato's expected ability to fight. In 1945, the Yamato participated in its final battle known as Operation Ten-Go. During this the Yamato accompanied by a small escort fleet was sent to attack American forces that were landing on Okinawa. When the Yamato was spotted by an American Carrier Task Force, it was attacked by many waves of aircraft before it was finally sunk by a torpedo. The sinking of the Yamato marked the end of an era of the dominance of battleships in naval warfare as the use of aircraft carriers became more practical and effective.
