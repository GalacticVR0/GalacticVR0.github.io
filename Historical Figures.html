WWII Historical Figures

Winston Churchill

Winston Churchill was an extremely successful and powerful political leader but held office for the majority of World War II. He gained an immense amount of popularity both during and after the war because of his strategic approaches and how to end it. Elected in 1940, he began by motivating Britain to remain vigilant even while Germany swept through Europe. Utilizing his persuasiveness as an asset, he used propaganda to encourage Britain to begin to defend itself against the German air raids and think about beginning an assault on the Nazi occupied Europe. Due to many of Churchill's public speeches, recruitment rose to over 24 percent and in 1944, the number jumped to over 33 percent. During the war, he worked alongside the United States President Franklin D. Roosevelt and the Soviet leader Joseph Stalin to develop strategies on how to reclaim Europe. From this, the D-Day invasion was created and although there were many casualties from the assault, it still helped start the invasion by providing a protected landing and staging area for assets. Overall, Winston Churchill provided a major contribution to the war and was one of the reasons why the Allies ultimately won the war.

Adolf Hitler

Adolf Hitler was the leader of Germany from 1933 to 1945 and during this time, he forced his worldviews of territorial expansion and racial supremacy beginning with Germany before spreading it to the majority of Europe. After the loss of World War I, Hitler began his rise in political power and in a little under 15 years had climbed all the way up to establishing his dictatorship in 1933. Within a few days Hitler began removing any ideology that he did not want in his regime. When Britain rejected the peace treaty offered after Germany invaded Poland, Hitler decided on capturing Norway and Denmark before Britain would send forces to protect them. When in the following months Britain still did not accept a peace treaty, Hitler began to think about an invasion of their country. At the same time, he ordered the invasion of the Soviet Union. His strategy was to try and declare peace with as many countries as he could while conquering those that would not, before breaking the peace treaties. However, this strategy was soon eliminated when Japan bombed Pearl Harbor and Germany had to declare war on the United States. Overall, Adolf Hitler was a powerful and theatrical person who with more humane and noble ideas could have made a much better impact on the world.
